Teaching Philosophy

As an instructor, I take great influence from the way I situate my classroom from Randy Pausch's The Last Lecture. While I will always be biased reading from a fellow Pittsburgh native, this book has stuck with me to realize the importance of leaving a legacy that I am not only passionate about, but one that enables the legacy of others.

With my teaching philosophy, I live by this thought process. My goal is not just to effectively teach the materials on hand but to position them for students to realize the practicality of writing going into the legacy of their professional futures. I work to make an active connection between a student's future workplace and the foundational rhetorical skills that they might not realize play a role in how they conduct themselves professionally. Whether that is using a resume and cover letter as a rhetorical situation example or using standard workplace conventions as a conversation about audience analysis, I force this level transfer for students to make these connections not only in the classroom, but when they are at their future desks in a workplace setting.

Through this, I also stand firm on treating my classroom as an open environment where everyone can learn. As cliche as that sounds, I truly feel that students and instructors learn together, regardless of the material being presented. I emphasize to my students from the first day that we will learn together as the course goes on throughout the semester. Whether I am learning a bit about each of my students or they bring up an interesting topic that I might not have considered, all parties involved learn together. The old hierarchical belief that an instructor is the all-knowing being in a classroom is not the legacy that I aim to follow. The moment an instructor acts as this overly enforced character is the moment they lose the class in my eyes, and is one thing I push to avoid at all costs.

My studies in User Experience (UX) and Document Design aid me in how I present the literal legacy materials to my class. From a physical perspective, these two emphases allow me to utilize emerging technologies to further connect my students with the coursework and each other. Using actual workplace software or conventions forces transfer on students that can resonate with them when they reach their first position in industry. From a pedagogical perspective, this allows me to tailor my materials in an attempt to reach out to what each student aspires to be. Whether they are an Accounting major who focuses on answers through hard-hitting numbers or a Kinesiology major who might focus on the actions behind their words, they can still come together and realize the power of rhetorical thinking that a professional document could hold.

My teaching philosophy is ultimately a legacy of embracing connectivity, community, and practicality. I want to continue learning from each class I teach and use my workplace experience as a bridge for students to see the value behind the course content. This is the reason I became an instructor, and seeing students realize the true power that writing and rhetorical thinking hold both in the classroom and at their future positions is what I deem as a successful legacy.
